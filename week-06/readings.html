Reading Reflections

Creative Prototyping · Week 6 Assignment

Assignment Overview

This page documents reflections from two readings on the design process and prototyping. Each quote below resonated with me through its perspective on iteration, systems thinking, and the evolving dialogue between code, creativity, and design.

Programming Design Systems

by Rune Madsen

“However, there is a century-long bond between the field of design and new advances in technology, and if graphic designers do not become fluent in this new digital reality, they will become irrelevant. We now have the ability to write code that produces beautiful designs, and the designer of tomorrow will have to understand how to deliver on that promise.”

This one hit me like a ton of bricks, but in a brutally refreshing way that feels validating. I oftentimes thought about this in my own experiences in the last few years, as I saw the coming of adaptive and flexibile systems affecting traditional roles and models of working.

“... it enables designers to create dynamic systems that can change their designs based on time, place, or use. Throwing a design over the wall for production is a bad legacy of the printed page, and there is no reason for the design process to end with the birth of a product.”

Love this. Using programming languages as a powerful tool to empower designers naturally feels like the next step of evolution that more and more designers are taking on.

“There is a need for educational material that teaches the fundamentals of graphc design in a modern way, especially if it also gives students mental models for critiquing digital design projects.”

100% agree. It's a necessicty now, not an afterthought. There seems to be less and less excuse for digital designers to feel pitted against engineers, coders and the rest of the dev teams, and more of a reason to blend programming into graphic design practices.

What Do Prototypes Prototype?

by Houde and Hill

“Choosing the right kind of more focused prototype to build is an art in itself, and communicating its limited purposes to its various audiences is a critical aspect of its use.”

Ironically, this is the least visible and least communicated step I've experienced when working on my own as well as when I've collaborated with others.

“Limited understanding of design practice on the part of supporting organizations makes it hard for designers to explain their prototypes to them. Finally, prototypes are not self-explanitory: looks can be decieving. Clarifying what aspects of a prototype correspond to the eventual artifact-andw what don't-is a key part of successful prototyping.”

This scenario has been at times, a confusing obstacle and huge pain point for me. Having this article would've saved so much headache.

“To design well, designers must be willing to use different tools for different prototyping tasks; and to team up with other people with complementary skills.”

Simple but powerful. Pushing past potentially limiting tools and mediums into a multi-sensory level of exploration of a problem is hard work, but necessary for greater design impact.
